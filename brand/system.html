The color and typography system for Burner, which lets your AI assistant use a real Android phone for you. It's built around the Wink Phone logo: a plain touchscreen phone whose orange screen winks back at you. Cool ink and paper neutrals do the quiet work, and the logo's orange is the one loud color.

Analysis

What the system has to carry

Personality

A capable stand-in with good manners

  • Direct. You ask, your assistant does it on a real phone.
  • Scrappy. "Burner" means a disposable device, a little underground.
  • Trustworthy. It's acting on someone's phone, so it has to look in control.
Audience

People who already work through an AI assistant

  • Heavy assistant users who want it to handle app-only tasks.
  • Builders wiring phone access into their own agents.
  • Early adopters who find tools on X and GitHub, not trade shows.
Where it shows up

Mostly inside someone else's app

  • In the assistant's chat, as a small card showing what it's doing on the phone.
  • useburner.si and the web installer.
  • GitHub README in light and dark mode.
  • Social cards, favicon, stickers.
Implication

Be recognizable at chip size

  • Burner has to read as Burner inside Claude, ChatGPT and other assistants' UI, on light and dark.
  • The status colors carry the story: working, done, needs you.
  • One color and a wordmark must be enough; there's no room for more.

Competitor hues are approximate, sampled from memory to show territory rather than exact brand values. Burn is the logo's orange. It is brighter and more saturated than Claude's clay, and it never appears without the ink phone shape, which keeps it apart from BrowserStack's orange.

Primary palette

Ink, Slate, Wire, Mist, Paper

The neutrals do almost all the work. They are the logo's own ink and paper, extended into a short cool-grey ramp, so the orange always has something calm to sit on.

Accent palette

Burn, plus three signals

Burn is the logo's orange and the brand's one loud color. Pulse, Tether and Fault are status colors that map one-to-one to what the assistant is doing on the phone.

Usage rules

How to use it, and how not to

Do

  1. One Burn moment per view.The wink icon, the wordmark underscore, the connect button. If two things are Burn, neither is.
  2. Put Ink text on Burn.5.9:1. White on Burn is only 2.9, so it fails body text.
  3. Use Burn Deep for links on light grounds.Burn itself is 2.6 on Paper; Burn Deep is 5.1.
  4. Let orange fill only a phone screen.The logo is the one place Burn covers a whole shape. Everywhere else it's a button, an underscore or an icon.
  5. Set step labels and status tags in Martian Mono.Small caps labels only. Never sentences.
  6. Keep headlines short and lowercase-friendly.Lowercase matches the wordmark. Four to six words per line at display size.

Don't

  1. Don't use Burn as a background block.Large fields of it read as an error or a sale. Keep it under 5% of any surface.
  2. Don't use Fault for branding, or Burn for errors.Status colors only mean status.
  3. Don't set Burn or Wire as text on light grounds.2.6:1 and 2.4:1 on Paper. Use Burn Deep or Slate instead.
  4. Don't use green for success.Android owns that green. Tether teal does the job and stays ours.
  5. Don't use the >_ face outside the phone.On its own it reads as a command prompt. The wink always lives inside the phone outline.
  6. Don't set body copy in Bricolage or Martian Mono.Paragraphs are always Atkinson Hyperlegible Next.

Mix by placement

Website hero

Paper ground, Ink type, Mist strips, Burn on the button and the logo.

Docs and installer

Paper ground, Slate secondary text, Burn Deep links.

Card in the assistant

Ink or Paper to match the host's theme, status colors only for status.

Social card

Ink ground, Paper headline, the wink icon. It stands out in a feed of white cards.
